# Phase 45 Literature and Strategy-Definition Review

The strategy-builder universe visible in the supplied screenshots matches a common 30-preset grouping covering Bullish, Bearish, Neutral and Other structures.

Source: https://stockmojo.in/feature/option-strategy-builder/

Batman is commonly defined as a call ratio spread combined with a put ratio spread, producing two profit peaks and undefined tail risk in the standard version.

Sources: https://strikevue.com/options-strategies/batman and https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/how-to-trade-the-batman-strategy-using-nxtoption

Double Plateau is described as a defined-risk extension of the dual-peak Batman concept, commonly formed from a lower put condor and upper call condor.

Sources: https://www.strikevue.com/options-strategies/double_plateau and https://theoptionsguide.com/double-plateau.aspx

Jade Lizard combines a short OTM put with a short call spread; the defining construction aims to collect credit at least as large as the call-spread width to remove upside loss, leaving downside exposure.

Source: https://www.investopedia.com/the-jade-lizard-option-strategy-7229206

Reverse Jade Lizard mirrors the construction with a short call plus a short put spread, aiming to remove downside risk when the collected credit covers the put-spread width.

Source: https://www.quantsapp.com/learn/option-strategies/reverse-jade-lizard

Range Forward and Risk Reversal are closely related directional two-leg constructions. A bullish range forward commonly buys an OTM call and sells an OTM put; the bearish mirror buys an OTM put and sells an OTM call.

Sources: https://www.strikevue.com/options-strategies/range_forward and https://www.optionsacademy.org/strategies/risk-reversal/

Bull/Bear Condors and Bull/Bear Butterflies are defined-risk directional structures targeting a specified price/range rather than an unbounded move.

Sources: https://www.quantsapp.com/learn/option-strategies/bull-call-condor, https://www.quantsapp.com/learn/option-strategies/bear-put-condor, https://www.strikevue.com/options-strategies/bull_butterfly, and https://www.strikevue.com/options-strategies/bear_condor

Strip and Strap are asymmetric long-straddle variants: Strip uses one call and two puts; Strap uses two calls and one put.

Source: https://wiki.fcontext.com/options/strip-and-strap/

These references are used only to lock the fixed leg definitions before numerical testing. The study does not assume textbook preferred regimes are valid for NIFTY; that is tested empirically.
